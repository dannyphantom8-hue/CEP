Mumbai's coast has a root system.

Mangrove forests line the creeks and shores of Mumbai. They soften storm waves, shelter young fish and hold the mud together. Move the tide below to see what happens among the roots.

Why the roots matter

A wave breaker

Dense, tangled roots slow incoming water. Coasts with mangroves take less damage during cyclones and storm surges, which matters in a city built on reclaimed islands.

A fish nursery

Young fish, prawns and crabs hide among the roots until they are big enough for open water. Many of the fish that reach local markets began life here.

A carbon store

Mangrove mud locks away carbon for a very long time. Cutting a mangrove releases part of that store back into the air.

A bird habitat

Creeks near the city, such as Thane Creek, draw large flocks of migratory birds, including flamingos, to feed on the mudflats.

What is hurting them

  • Land cleared or filled in for construction and roads
  • Garbage and plastic washing in with the tide and tangling in the roots
  • Sewage and untreated waste flowing into creeks
  • Debris dumping that buries the breathing roots

Most of this is visible to anyone who walks along a creek. That is why local people who notice and report damage matter so much.

Who lives here

MudskipperA fish that climbs out onto the mud at low tide and breathes air through its skin.
Fiddler crabMales wave one oversized claw to attract mates. Look for them on exposed mud.
FlamingoFlocks feed in shallow creeks and mudflats in the cooler months.
KingfisherPerches on branches over the water and dives for small fish.
Avicennia (grey mangrove)A common local tree. Its pencil-like roots poke up from the mud to take in air.

Where to see mangroves near Mumbai

Sewri mudflats

Known for flocks of flamingos in the cooler months. Bring binoculars and keep a respectful distance.

Thane Creek

A creek with wide mangrove belts and a protected flamingo area on its shores.

Mahim Nature Park

A city green space beside a mangrove-lined creek. A gentle place to start learning species.

Airoli and Gorai creeks

Creek-side stretches where guided walks and boat trips are sometimes offered. Check current timings first.

Rules for any visit: stay on paths, never pick roots or branches, carry out all litter, and keep noise low near birds.

About this project

Why it exists

Mangroves protect Mumbai's coast, yet many residents never see them up close. This site turns that gap into short, friendly lessons.

What you can do

Explore the tide, learn the species, test yourself, plan a visit, and take a personal pledge you can print.

How it is built

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript in one file. No tracking, no sign-in, and it works offline once opened.
